# Canvas Design

Creates museum- and magazine-quality visual art as PNG or PDF through intentional design philosophy.

## Two-Phase Process

### Phase 1: Design Philosophy

Write a manifesto (4–6 paragraphs) articulating a visual movement expressed through:
- Form and geometry
- Space and negative space
- Color relationships
- Compositional rhythm

The philosophy should feel like it comes from an imaginary design discipline. It guides aesthetic direction while leaving interpretive space.

**Philosophy examples:**
- *Concrete Poetry* — "Communication through monumental form and bold geometry"
- *Chromatic Language* — "Color as the primary information system"
- *Analog Meditation* — "Quiet visual contemplation through texture"
- *Geometric Silence* — "Pure order and restraint"

### Phase 2: Canvas Expression

Translate the philosophy into the output artifact. The design is the communication — not the explanation.

**Core principle:** "Information lives in design, not paragraphs."

## Standards

- Text is always minimal and visual-first
- No overlapping elements without intentional purpose
- Breathing room and clear separation between elements
- Compositions should feel like artifacts from an imagined discipline
- No amateurish or cartoony execution

## Craftsmanship Mandate

Work must appear:
- Meticulously crafted and painstakingly refined
- As if produced by someone at the absolute top of their field
- Museum or magazine quality — not decorated documents

Polish through refinement, not addition. Make existing compositions more cohesive before adding new elements.

## Output

- Format: `.png` or `.pdf`
- Canvas boundaries must contain all elements cleanly
- Typography, color palette, and spacing treated as primary design decisions
- Final work should appear as though countless hours were invested in it
